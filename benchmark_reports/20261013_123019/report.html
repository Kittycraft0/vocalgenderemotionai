Suggestions, most important first

PRIORITY 1

Log the time each value describes, not the end of its window

Evidence. Your values describe audio about 30 ms before the logged timestamp. Comparing logs to a dataset at face value gives 48.5% pitch accuracy instead of 85.0%. Formants within 10% drop from 94.2% to 62.8% (F1).

What to change. Each feature uses a different amount of recent audio: pitch the last 50 ms, formants the last 100 ms (read at the middle, so 50 ms back), vocal weight the last 256 samples. In main11's logger, store the window centre per feature (for example pitch_time = t - 0.025, formant_time = t - 0.05), or shift audio_timestamp back by a single agreed amount. Any dataset comparison should use these times.

PRIORITY 1

Add a real voicing check instead of loudness + zero-crossing gates

Evidence. 18.6% of frames with no voice still get a pitch (Praat 3.4%). Your get_pitch only checks loudness (RMS > 0.001) and zero-crossing rate, and librosa.yin always returns some pitch, so any quiet hum or breath that passes both gates gets a pitch. 57% of these frames are within 50 ms of real voicing, where the analysis window still holds the start or end of a voiced sound. The typical one is only 20 dB below the loudest frame, so it is not quiet noise. A loudness gate tied to each recording's noise floor barely helps (voicing error 14.8% at best becomes 14.1%), so the fix has to look at periodicity.

What to change. Add a periodicity test: librosa.pyin's voiced probability, Praat's voicing threshold, or YIN's own aperiodicity value (reject frames above about 0.2-0.3). Judge voicing on the newest 20-30 ms rather than the whole 50 ms window so it switches off sooner after a vowel ends, and require 2-3 voiced frames in a row before reporting a pitch to remove one-frame blips.

PRIORITY 1

Stop using zero-crossing rate to reject noisy frames

Evidence. Pitch accuracy falls to 63.0% at 10 dB and 35.5% at 5 dB SNR. At 10 dB, 100% of the voiced frames you miss were thrown out by the zero-crossing gate, because noise adds zero crossings even when a voice is clearly there. The candidate fix (Praat on the same 100 ms window) keeps 78.5% at 10 dB.

What to change. Replace the ZCR > 0.15 rule with a periodicity measure (see the voicing suggestion). For live use, a microphone with noise suppression off plus a periodicity-based gate is more reliable than ZCR.

PRIORITY 2

Widen the pitch search range

Evidence. In the PTDB recordings, 482 voiced frames are below 75 Hz (deep or creaky male voices), and only 9.1% of them get a correct pitch. Synthetic voices at 700, 900, 1200 Hz come out an octave low (half the true pitch). get_pitch searches 75.0-600.0 Hz.

What to change. Use fmin around 55-60 Hz and fmax around 1000-1100 Hz (your notes mention singing up to 1000-2000 Hz). YIN needs at least two periods of the lowest pitch in its frame, so pass frame_length explicitly (about 2.5/fmin seconds of samples) instead of librosa's default of 2048 samples, which is a different duration at every sample rate.

PRIORITY 2

Smooth out octave jumps over time

Evidence. 4.7% of your voiced frames are more than 20% off (Praat 2.1%), 1.7% are octave jumps. Each frame is estimated on its own, so nothing stops a single frame from jumping an octave.

What to change. Keep the last few pitch values and reject a new value that jumps by about an octave unless the jump persists for 2-3 frames (or run a median filter over the last 3-5 frames). pYIN and Praat do this with a Viterbi path. This per-frame error data is also exactly what the correction network idea (learning true values from recent frames) would train on.

PRIORITY 2

Rebuild the base spectral slope

Evidence. It barely reacts to voice brightness (rank correlation +0.10, total change 0.9 across a 12 dB/octave range), while the derived slope tracks it perfectly (+1.00). The fit runs over every FFT bin up to 4500 Hz with a -60 dB floor, so the many high, quiet bins and the floor dominate the line.

What to change. Fit dB against log-frequency on band energies (for example octave bands from 100 to 4000 Hz) or use a standard measure such as the alpha ratio (energy 50-1000 Hz vs 1-5 kHz) or the Hammarberg index. Or drop it and keep the derived slope.

PRIORITY 2

Make vocal weight independent of pitch, loudness and sample rate

Evidence. Changing pitch moves vocal weight 1.1x as much as the full brightness range (loudness 0.43x, sample rate 0.29x). It also jumps 29% of its useful range from one frame to the next on a perfectly steady vowel. It analyses only 256 samples (5.3 ms at 48 kHz, 16 ms at 16 kHz), which is less than one pitch period for most voices, and log(energy + 1) depends on the input level.

What to change. Use a fixed duration (25-40 ms) instead of a fixed sample count, resample to one rate first, normalise the frame's level before taking logs, and average over a few frames. Then re-run this benchmark: the pitch and loudness ratios should fall well below 0.5.

PRIORITY 3

Choose the formant ceiling from the voice instead of always 5500 Hz

Evidence. The Praat ceiling sweep picks men 5000 Hz, women 5500 Hz, boys 5500 Hz, girls 5500 Hz. A ceiling chosen from the detected pitch (the candidate fix) raises F3 within-10% for girls by 4.5 points, but lowers F3 for boys by 1.6. Averaged over F1-F3 for everyone the change is +0.8 points. Pitch alone is a rough guide to vocal tract length, which is what the ceiling should follow.

What to change. Rather than a fixed pitch rule, try 2-3 ceilings in get_formants_praat and keep the one whose formant tracks are smoothest over the last few frames (the Escudero et al. 2009 method). Let a user setting override it, since a trained voice can have a high pitch with a long vocal tract.

PRIORITY 3

Fix the spectral slope unit on the dashboard

Evidence. The slope is fitted against log10(frequency), so it is in dB per decade (per 10x in frequency). The dashboard readout says dB/kHz.

What to change. Change the label to dB/decade, or divide by 3.32 and call it dB/octave, the unit most voice research uses.

PRIORITY 3

Check the breath (noise) rolloff

Evidence. Adding breath noise barely changes it (rank correlation +0.20), so it does not measure breathiness yet. The helper named for an 85% rolloff actually uses 98%.

What to change. Use harmonics-to-noise ratio (Praat's to_harmonicity, or the cepstral peak prominence CPP) as the breathiness measure. Both are standard and have published norms.

PRIORITY 3

The 2-second noise calibration currently has no effect

Evidence. get_audio_data receives noise_power_profile but never uses it (the denoised harmonic function is commented out). The live mode waits 2 s at startup for nothing.

What to change. Either use it, for example to set the voicing loudness gate from the room's noise level (see above), or skip the recording.

Datasets, method and limits

  • PTDB-TUG, Pirker et al., Interspeech 2011. Read English sentences from 10 female and 10 male speakers, with laryngograph-based reference pitch every 10 ms. Open Database License. spsc.tugraz.at
  • Hillenbrand et al. 1995, JASA 97(5). 1,668 vowel recordings at 16 kHz from 45 men, 48 women and 46 children. MIT license, hosted with the author's permission. github.com/santiagobarreda/hillenbrand_et_al_1995
  • Synthetic vowels built in benchmark/bench_datasets.py: a harmonic source with exact spectral tilt through four formant resonators.

Metrics. Correct = within 50 cents (half a semitone) of the reference. Gross error = more than 20% off. Voicing error = frames where voiced/unvoiced is called wrong. Formants: share of vowels within 10% of the measured value, and the median error. Each tool's time alignment is found by testing shifts and keeping the best one.

Limits. Hillenbrand's formants were measured with LPC, the same family of method Praat and your code use, so formant scores may look better than they would against X-ray or MRI data. The laryngograph reference marks creaky voice as voiced, which no audio-only tracker handles well. Noise here is white noise only. Live microphones add automatic gain control and room echo, which is not tested. Only a subset of PTDB-TUG is used; raise --ptdb-per-speaker for more.
